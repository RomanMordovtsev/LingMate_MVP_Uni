## Principles for LingMate

### 1. Motivation is designed, not assumed
- Dörnyei's L2 Motivational Self System: Ideal L2 Self (who I want to be in the language), Ought-to L2 Self (external expectations), L2 Learning Experience (the immediate learning environment). Motivation is dynamic and fluctuates with tasks and context (Dörnyei & Ushioda 2021).
- Self-Determination Theory: intrinsic vs extrinsic; autonomy, competence, relatedness.
- **Design rule:** onboarding asks *why* the learner studies and *who they want to be* in the language (living abroad, travelling, career, partner/family, culture, exam). Scenarios, examples and progress messages are built from that answer. Show growth as competence ("now you can…"), give the learner choices (autonomy), give the mascot a warm relationship role (relatedness).

### 2. Enjoyment and anxiety are separate levers
- Dewaele et al. (2016): Foreign Language Enjoyment and Foreign Language Classroom Anxiety are two different dimensions, not opposites. Enjoyment comes from interesting tasks, pride in achievement, a supportive atmosphere.
- Anxiety (Horwitz; MacIntyre) lowers willingness to communicate (WTC), one of the strongest predictors of actual L2 use.
- **Design rule:** private, judgement-free practice by default (no public leaderboards); humour and surprise (Yuji); explicit moments of pride. Never shame for mistakes or broken streaks.

### 3. The learner is an LX user, not a "deficient native speaker"
- Dewaele, Bak & Ortega (2021): drop the NS/NNS deficit framing; use L1 user / LX user.
- **Design rule:** progress is framed by what the learner can do (CEFR can-do), never by distance from a native speaker. Intelligibility and successful communication beat accent perfection.

### 4. Multilingual learners transfer from all their languages
- Ortega (2009, ch. 3): transfer affects rate, avoidance, frequency and overgeneralisation; psychotypology (perceived closeness) matters; L3 learners transfer from L1 *and* L2s.
- Dmitrenko (2017): multilingual adults use specific strategies drawing on earlier languages.
- **Design rule:** the profile stores all the learner's languages and levels (Roman: RU L1, EN C1, IT A1). Explanations can use the closest known language ("like Italian *stare*…"). Track **avoidance** too: a structure the learner never produces (e.g. subjunctive) is a signal, not an absence of errors.

### 5. Fossilization / stabilization
- Han (2004): persistent non-target forms despite ability, opportunity and motivation; causes include L1, age, input, and *communicative sufficiency* (the message gets across, so there is no pressure to change).
- **Design rule:** detect errors that persist over weeks in the error log and escalate: more explicit feedback, targeted practice, pushed output. Because LLM tutors understand broken language easily, LingMate must create communicative *need* for accuracy (see morphosyntax.md §2–3).

### 6. Explicit knowledge needs proceduralisation
- Macaro & Masterman (2006): an intensive explicit grammar course improved some grammar-test scores but not accuracy in translation or free writing.
- Lightbown (2003): communicative input alone is not enough for accuracy; some L1–L2 contrasts need negative evidence (Trahey & White 1993); practice helps when it is meaningful, not mechanical; developmental stages cannot be skipped.
- **Design rule:** every explanation is immediately followed by productive use in a meaningful exchange. Measure progress on free production, not quizzes.

### 7. Age and adult learners
- Age-of-acquisition effects on ultimate attainment are real (Bylund et al. 2021; Hartshorne et al. 2018 — grammar learning ability declines after ~17), but adults are faster in early stages, and in instructed settings motivation, amount and quality of input matter more (Pfenninger & Singleton 2016).
- **Design rule:** for adult users, maximise meaningful input and interaction time, use explicit help, and set realistic goals (functional proficiency, not native-likeness).

### 8. Individual differences
- Aptitude (Carroll: phonemic coding, inductive learning, grammatical sensitivity, associative memory) predicts *rate* more than final level (Li & Zhao 2021).
- Learning strategies (Oxford): cognitive, metacognitive (planning, monitoring, evaluating), social/affective.
- **Design rule:** adapt pace to observed learning rate; support metacognition with weekly reflection ("what was hard, what will you try next week").

### 9. Developmental sequences
- Brown's morpheme order; stages of negation and questions in L1 and L2 (course handouts).
- **Design rule:** estimate the learner's stage from their utterances; correct what they are ready for.
