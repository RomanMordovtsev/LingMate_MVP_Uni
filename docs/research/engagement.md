# Engagement and habit design

LingMate should be something people *want* to open every day, without sacrificing learning. Combine SLA motivation research (docs/research/sla.md §1–3) with industry practice.

## Evidence and practice
- **Streaks work, with care.** A randomized field experiment with ~60,000 students (Northwestern IPR working paper WP-26-05, 2026, math app in Peru) found that highlighting streaks raised weekly use, similar to personalised reminders, with suggestive learning gains and no discouragement effect after breaks. Khan Academy retired streaks over concerns about demotivation when breaks are outside the learner's control.
  Source: https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf
- **Duolingo retention practice** (public talks and write-ups): streaks with protection (streak freeze/repair), daily goals with explicit commitment wording, personalised practice reminders, mascot emotion (e.g. widget where the mascot's mood changes), streak milestones as identity markers.
  Sources: https://lazyweb.com/research/duolingo-retention-experiments , https://mobbin.com/blog/streaks-are-about-losing-a-study-of-859-streak-designs
- **Loss aversion cuts both ways:** streaks motivate through fear of losing them, which can feel coercive. Prefer forgiving mechanics.

## Design rules
1. **Tiny daily commitment.** Default goal = 1 short session (5–10 min, e.g. one scenario or one review round). The learner sets it; never more than they chose.
2. **Forgiving streak.** Daily streak with 1–2 automatic "rest days" per week (or a weekly-streak option). Missing a day never wipes visible progress (total days, can-dos, words learned stay).
3. **Yuji the mascot is the relationship.** Yuji greets, reacts to effort (not just correctness), celebrates milestones, occasionally shares a short "how languages are learned" fact. Mood/animations are encouraging, never guilt-tripping.
4. **Personal reminders.** Learner picks reminder time; reminders reference their own goal and next real-world task ("Mañana tienes la cita en el banco — ¿practicamos 5 minutos?"). Opt-in; easy to snooze.
5. **Progress as competence.** Celebrate can-do milestones and real-life wins ("you booked a doctor's appointment in Spanish"), not XP alone. XP/points, if any, reward meaningful output (completed tasks, self-corrections), not taps.
6. **Session shape.** Warm-up review (1–2 min) → main task → quick wrap-up with 1 highlight and 1 thing to remember → next session teaser.
7. **Variety and surprise.** Rotate scenario types, occasional surprise events inside scenarios (the café ran out of what you ordered), occasional bonus content tied to interests.
8. **No public competition by default.** Leaderboards can raise anxiety (sla.md §2); if ever added, opt-in and friend-based.
9. **Measure.** Track D1/D7/D30 retention, sessions per week, and learning metrics (accuracy per error category) together — engagement that doesn't move learning is not success.
