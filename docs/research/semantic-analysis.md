## Principles for LingMate

### 1. Three sources of word meaning
- Ellis (2026): a word's meaning comes from **signification** (word ↔ concept/referent; Saussure 1916; embodied cognition), **lexical company** (Firth 1957: "you shall know a word by the company it keeps"; corpus linguistics) and **social usage** (Wittgenstein 1953: meaning is use). The learner fuses the three over many encounters.
- **Design rule:** a vocabulary item in LingMate has all three: a meaning in the learner's L1 / an image (signification), 2–3 typical collocations or frames (company), and a short situation where it is used (usage). A translation alone is not "knowing the word".

### 2. The phrase, not the word, is the usual unit of meaning
- Sinclair (1991, 2004): idiom principle — most text is built from semi-preconstructed phrases; Pawley & Syder (1983): retrieving whole chunks frees attention for planning; Bybee (2005): words used together fuse together.
- **Design rule:** SRS cards and curriculum chunks are multi-word units ("pedir cita", "darse cuenta de", "¿me cobras?") with their usual grammar, not isolated lemmas. Consistent with syllabus-design §2.

### 3. Frequency, contingency, prototypicality
- Ellis et al. (2016); Ellis (2002): frequency of use, how reliably a form predicts a meaning, and prototypical exemplars drive L1 and L2 learning of constructions; frequent words have many delexicalised, phrase-bound senses (Sinclair 1991, *back*).
- **Design rule:** pick vocabulary by frequency in the learner's situations, introduce the prototypical sense first, and teach high-frequency polysemous words (*dar, quedar, llevar, echar*) through their main frames rather than one gloss.

### 4. Knowing the name is not knowing the thing
- Ellis (2026): L2 learners' grasp of morphosyntax and of abstract/low-frequency words lags behind simple word–translation mapping (Ellis 2022; Klein 1998). Full understanding of specialised words needs the community of practice where they are used.
- **Design rule:** for relocation vocabulary (bank, padrón, health centre) the tutor checks understanding in a task ("what will you bring to the appointment?"), not by asking for a translation.

### 5. L1 entrenchment and the dialectic of feedback
- Ellis (2006), the associative-cognitive CREED: learning is Construction-based, Rational, Exemplar-driven, Emergent and Dialectic; L1-tuned attention limits L2 learning, and explicit form-focused feedback (linguistic, pragmatic or metalinguistic) helps overcome it.
- **Design rule:** supports existing choices — transfer maps (morphosyntax §7) and noticeable feedback (morphosyntax §2).

### 6. LLMs know company, not use
- Ellis (2026), Mahowald et al. (2024): LLMs are strong at formal linguistic competence (lexical company) and weaker at functional competence grounded in the world and social use.
- **Design rule:** Yuji's word explanations come with a quick check against a curated source where it matters (frequency list, dictionary entry or Roman's confirmation) and are logged for the eval harness; don't let the LLM invent collocations for the curriculum.

### 7. Researching vocabulary (course unit 4, to complete)
- Ellis (2026) recommends corpus exploration (COCA / english-corpora.org; for Spanish, the equivalent corpora) of concordances and collocates.
- **Design rule:** later, a "word profile" view built from a Spanish corpus (collocates, frequency band) for words the learner looks up. Needs a data source Roman approves.
