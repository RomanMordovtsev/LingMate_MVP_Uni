## Principles for LingMate

### 1. Plurilingual, not monolingual, design
- CEFR plurilingualism: the aim is a repertoire where all languages interact; partial competences are valuable. Multicompetence (Cook) — a multilingual is not "several monolinguals".
- Cenoz & Gorter (2013): soften the boundaries between languages; García & Sylvan (2011), Creese & Blackledge (2010), Galante et al. (2023, digital plurilingual pedagogies), Llanes & Cots (2020): translanguaging supports learning and engagement. Caveat — Treffers-Daller (2025) criticises over-strong translanguaging claims; keep terms precise.
- **Design rule:** the learner can use any language from their repertoire in the chat; explanations may draw on the closest known language (IT↔ES for Roman). Store the whole repertoire in the profile. Optional onboarding "language portrait" (Carbonara 2023).

### 2. Relocated learners are migrants — respect their L1 and identity
- Lising (2024) "multilingual mindset"; Lucido et al. (2024) affirming culture and identity.
- **Design rule:** never frame the L1 as a problem; include intercultural notes in scenarios with reflection prompts (Heggernes 2021: texts + dialogue + reflection build intercultural competence).

### 3. Being abroad is not enough
- Isabelli-García et al. (2018): study-abroad gains vary hugely with amount/quality of contact, social networks, identity, anxiety; fluency and pragmatics gain more than accuracy. Housen (2012): contact outside class matters.
- **Design rule:** "do it in real life" missions — prepare in the app, do the real task (bank, pharmacy, neighbour), debrief afterwards. Use the linguistic landscape around the learner (Gorter, Cenoz & van der Worp 2021): "photograph a sign, let's read it together".

### 4. Spacing beats cramming
- Serrano (2012): distributed practice is better for long-term retention.
- **Design rule:** short daily sessions + spaced repetition (engagement.md §1, roadmap 3.1).

### 5. Multilingual experience lowers anxiety and grows aptitude
- Thompson & Khawaja (2016): more languages → less FL anxiety. Huang et al. (2020): aptitude and working memory can grow with L2/L3 learning. Duñabeitia et al. (2014): no inhibitory "bilingual advantage" in children — don't overclaim cognitive benefits.
- **Design rule:** honest motivational messages (Yuji can say "language-learning ability grows with practice"), no pseudo-scientific promises.

### 6. Generative AI: evolution, not revolution
- Pegrum (2025): AI tutors can explain grammar, contextualise vocabulary, role-play and give feedback, but risk hallucinations, bias, over-reliance; AI literacy is needed. Gillespie (2020): CALL research lacks longitudinal studies.
- **Design rule:** be transparent that Yuji is an AI; verify correction quality with the evaluation harness (roadmap 5.3); occasionally encourage the learner to double-check and to talk to real people; keep a human in the loop for anything high-stakes (e.g. official documents).

### 7. Different user groups
- Older learners (Puebla et al. 2022; later-life reviews): value clear usefulness, simple UI, technical confidence, social support.
- Workplace multilingualism (van der Worp et al. 2017): work-goal users need emails, calls, meetings.
- **Design rule:** goal-driven scenario packs (relocation, work, travel, hobby); accessibility settings (font size, slower TTS); no pressure mechanics for users who opt out.
