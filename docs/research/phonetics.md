## Principles for a future pronunciation module

### 1. Similar sounds are harder than new ones
- Contrastive Analysis predicted "different = difficult"; Wode's Crucial Similarity Measure and Flege's Speech Learning Model (SLM) show the opposite: L2 sounds *similar* to an L1 sound get classified as equivalent ("equivalence classification") and stay accented; clearly *new* sounds can form new categories.
- **Design rule:** prioritise L2 sounds that are similar-but-different from the learner's L1 categories.

### 2. Perception before production
- SLM: production accuracy is limited by perception; new categories form only if the learner perceives the difference.
- PAM-L2 (Best & Tyler 2007): how L2 contrasts assimilate to L1 categories (two-category, category-goodness, single-category, uncategorised) predicts discrimination difficulty; single-category assimilation is hardest.
- **Design rule:** start with perception training (minimal-pair listening, "which word did you hear?"), then production. Spend most effort on single-category contrasts.

### 3. Russian → Spanish priority list (to validate with Roman)
- Unstressed vowels: Russian reduces unstressed /o/, /a/, /e/ (akanye/ikanye); Spanish keeps full vowels → transfer of reduction is very likely.
- Tap /ɾ/ vs trill /r/ (pero/perro, caro/carro): Russian /r/ is not contrastive this way. Amengual (2016): L2 learners keep the contrast mainly through **duration** with 0–1 occlusions → assess duration, don't demand a perfect multi-tap trill.
- Palatalisation: Russian soft/hard consonant contrast doesn't exist in Spanish; avoid over-palatalising before /e, i/.
- Voiced stops vs approximants: Spanish /b d g/ → [β ð ɣ] between vowels.
- Stress-timed vs syllable-timed rhythm.

### 4. Age, input and use
- Oh et al. (2011): Japanese adults were initially more accurate than children but did not improve over a year in the US; children reached native-like vowels. Flege (2003): amount of L2 vs L1 use matters as much as length of residence. Muñoz & Llanes (2014): study abroad reduced foreign accent in both children and adults.
- **Design rule:** for adult (relocated) learners immersion alone is not enough; combine targeted perception/production practice with encouragement to use L2 daily. Intelligibility/comprehensibility is the goal, not native-likeness (sla.md §3).

### 5. Measurement (acoustics)
- Vowels: F1/F2 formants and duration; consonants: VOT, closure/occlusion counts, duration (Ladefoged & Johnson ch. 8; Praat practicals).
- **Design rule (later):** browser recording → server-side acoustic analysis (e.g. Praat-compatible library such as Parselmouth) for a few target contrasts; feedback as simple visuals ("your 'rr' is long enough ✔"). Needs Roman's approval (new dependency, privacy of voice data).
